# 6. Interviews, concept tests and usability sessions

Choose when observing real people can resolve a specific uncertainty. General meeting tools require a separate study plan, consent process and analysis; they are not testing panels.

| Candidate | When to consider it | Route to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Lookback](https://www.lookback.com/) | Contextual remote usability sessions and observation | B |
| [UserTesting](https://www.usertesting.com/) | Moderated or unmoderated human feedback studies | B |
| [Maze](https://maze.co/) | Prototype, concept or live-site research studies | B |
| [Lyssna](https://www.lyssna.com/) | Focused preference, first-click or navigation questions | B |
| [Useberry](https://www.useberry.com/) | Prototype usability tasks and behavior observations | B |
| [Ballpark](https://ballparkhq.com/) | Prototype, copy or concept feedback in one study | B |
| [Userlytics](https://www.userlytics.com/) | Remote human testing across digital experiences | B |
| [Zoom](https://www.zoom.com/) | Moderated interviews with your own recruited participants | B |
| [Google Meet](https://workspace.google.com/products/meet/) | Lightweight moderated sessions in an existing workspace | B |
| [Microsoft Teams](https://www.microsoft.com/microsoft-teams/group-chat-software) | Moderated internal or enterprise user sessions | B |

Use the [shared routing, capability limits and permissions](../tool-options.md#read-only-what-the-next-decision-needs) before acting.
