# 14. Participant recruitment, panels and scheduling

Choose when a study needs actual participants or appointment coordination. Panels and calendars serve different jobs; check target-population fit, consent, incentives and approved budget before outreach.

| Candidate | When to consider it | Route to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [User Interviews](https://www.userinterviews.com/) | Recruit and manage participants for a defined study | B |
| [Respondent](https://www.respondent.io/) | Recruit people matching professional or demographic criteria | B |
| [Prolific](https://www.prolific.com/) | Recruit a defined participant sample for structured research | B |
| [Great Question](https://www.greatquestion.com/) | Manage an existing research panel and study operations | B |
| [Ethnio](https://ethn.io/) | Customer intercepts, screening and participant operations | B / A? |
| [Rally](https://www.rallyuxr.com/) | Coordinate participant relationships and research operations | B |
| [Calendly](https://calendly.com/) | Book sessions with already-recruited participants | B |
| [Cal.com](https://cal.com/) | Scheduling with configurable booking workflows | B / A? |
| [Google Calendar](https://workspace.google.com/products/calendar/) | Coordinate availability in a Google Workspace team | B / A? |
| [Microsoft Bookings](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/business/scheduling-and-booking-app) | Appointment scheduling in a Microsoft workplace | B |

Use the [shared routing, capability limits and permissions](../tool-options.md#read-only-what-the-next-decision-needs) before acting.
