# Craft and artifact quality

Read when creating or refining the artifact in Develop/Deliver: a screen, flow, form, message, report, service policy or operational procedure. Use the agreed task and existing design system or policy; inspect what matters for this handoff, not every possible artifact.

Method completion, phase completion and the human's acceptance are separate decisions.
A tool being available does not establish access or authority to use it.
Proposed behavior and expected effects remain distinct from verified behavior and observed outcomes.

## Produce a usable artifact

Agree applicable quality criteria with the decision owner. Mark an irrelevant state not applicable with a reason; leave an uninspected aspect unknown. Do not treat a polished success path as a complete solution.

| Aspect | What to produce | Failure to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary task | A clear intended user/job, entry, success path and observable completion | Attractive output without a usable way to finish the task |
| Hierarchy | Put the next decision, essential information and consequence where they are legible first | Competing emphasis, hidden prerequisites or important content below distractions |
| Structure and IA | Group by the user's task; use meaningful labels and predictable navigation or document order | Internal terminology, ambiguous categories or an unexplained change of context |
| States | Specify applicable empty, loading, partial, error, permission-denied, offline, long-content, zero/one/many, destructive-confirmation and success behavior | Missing transitions, dead ends or a happy-path-only handoff |
| Edges and limits | Define inputs, boundaries, interruption/resume, repeated actions and recovery relevant to this task | Silent loss, duplicate effects or an unhandled boundary condition |
| Copy | Say what the user must understand before acting, what happened and how to recover; keep labels consistent | Vague actions, blame, hidden consequences or errors without a next step |
| Accessibility | Use [DS-15](methods/design-and-delivery.md#ds-15) for the applicable barriers and verification scope | Treating a quick inspection as conformance or disabled-user research |
| Consistency | Reuse established components, content patterns or written rules; explain deliberate departures | Accidental variation or a new pattern without a task reason |
| Rationale | Link choices, rejected alternatives, reasons and assumptions to the evidence available | A preference presented as evidence or a decision nobody can revisit |

For process/policy artifacts, cover the equivalent roles, triggers, rules, exception paths, handoffs, failure/recovery, accountable party and intended publication location. A proposed publication location does not authorize publishing there.

## Keep the rationale inspectable

Append a concise rationale record to the task journal; reuse an existing decision record rather than making a second document:

| Decision | Alternative rejected | Reason | Assumption it rests on | What would overturn it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Record the actual choice or mark it proposed | Preserve the alternative | Cite evidence or label judgment | Link its assumption ID if recorded | Name a relevant contradiction or review trigger |

Link that record from product memory when used; keep task history authoritative and do not invent prior decisions or acceptance. The human retains framing, priorities, solution selection and acceptance.

## Use specialist help when available

If the host exposes a suitable critique, UX-copy, design-system or data-visualization skill/tool, use it for the relevant step within actual access and authority. Record which one was used, on which artifact/version, and the scope of its result. Without it, inspect the same relevant criteria using available artifacts; do not skip the bar or install a new dependency solely to satisfy it.

This inspection is not usability evidence, accessibility conformance or acceptance, and does not establish any phase DoD by itself. Use [DS-16](methods/design-and-delivery.md#ds-16) for an expert walkthrough and [UXR](uxr.md) when observed user behavior is needed.
