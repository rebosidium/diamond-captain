Choose for the decision, not to complete a checklist. Reuse existing artifacts; no template or workshop is mandatory. These are synthesis, design, review and delivery methods, not primary qualitative/quantitative collection. For a new user study use [UXR](../uxr.md). Keep source observations, interpretations, hypotheses and decisions distinct. Team agreement, AI output and workshop ideas do not become user evidence. Best-fit modes are starting points; methods can support loops. [Design Council framework][framework].

## DS-01

**Method:** Evidence clustering. **Prefer when:** Existing findings are fragmented.
**Do:** Extract source-linked observations; group by recurring need or obstacle; name themes; retain contradictions and role differences.
**Output:** Themes linked to evidence and decisions affected.
**Needs/limits:** Accessible source material. Repeated excerpts are not independent people; clusters are interpretations. [Synthesis][synthesis].

## DS-02

**Method:** Alternative problem framing. **Prefer when:** A request embeds a preferred solution.
**Do:** Write plausible problem explanations; link supporting and conflicting evidence; frame a bounded, solution-open challenge; state what would overturn it.
**Output:** Chosen framing, rejected alternatives and unknowns.
**Needs/limits:** Existing evidence and a decision owner; reframing supplies no new observations. [Framing][framing].

## DS-03

**Method:** Outcome chain. **Prefer when:** Activity is being mistaken for impact.
**Do:** Connect proposed change → expected behaviour/service effect → desired outcome; expose assumptions; attach baseline, indicator, guardrail and review trigger.
**Output:** A testable change rationale and measurement needs.
**Needs/limits:** Agreed goal; arrows are causal hypotheses until supported. [Theory of change][change].

## DS-04

**Method:** Journey synthesis. **Prefer when:** Friction spans time, channels or organisations.
**Do:** Order existing episodes by stage; add actions, needs, touchpoints and breakdowns; separate materially different journeys; attach source locators.
**Output:** Current journey with gaps and intervention points.
**Needs/limits:** Observed episodes; invented stages/emotions are hypotheses. Do not merge incompatible experiences. [Experience mapping][journey].

## DS-05

**Method:** Service blueprint. **Prefer when:** A visible experience depends on hidden operations.
**Do:** Align journey steps with frontstage actions, backstage work and enabling systems; mark handoffs, owners and failure paths; distinguish current from proposed state.
**Output:** A delivery model locating weak links.
**Needs/limits:** Operational knowledge; unverified connections remain assumptions. [Service methods][service-methods].

## DS-06

**Method:** Dependency and ownership map. **Prefer when:** Another team, policy or supplier can block delivery.
**Do:** Trace each critical handoff to its input, provider, recipient, constraint and fallback; mark confirmed commitments versus unresolved dependencies.
**Output:** Dependency decisions with owners and triggers.
**Needs/limits:** Existing agreements or permitted access; a named owner is not their commitment. [Whole-service dependencies][alpha].

## DS-07

**Method:** Assumption risk map. **Prefer when:** Many unknowns compete for attention.
**Do:** List what must hold for value, feasibility and viability; compare consequence if false with evidence strength; select the smallest check for the riskiest unknown.
**Output:** Prioritised assumptions and next checks.
**Needs/limits:** Explicit consequences; ratings organise judgement rather than validate beliefs. [Risky assumptions][alpha].

## DS-08

**Method:** Mechanism divergence. **Prefer when:** Options differ only cosmetically.
**Do:** Keep the need fixed; vary who acts, timing, channel and degree of automation; include a service/process option and doing less; explain each mechanism and downside.
**Output:** Materially different solution hypotheses.
**Needs/limits:** A bounded problem; novelty and votes are not effectiveness evidence. [Develop][framework].

## DS-09

**Method:** Co-design. **Prefer when:** Affected people can change how options work.
**Do:** Share the need and constraints; let users/frontline staff make or alter alternatives; capture disagreements, contributions and design consequences.
**Output:** Co-created options and unresolved tensions.
**Needs/limits:** Authorized participation, accessible materials and clear decision rights; suggestions do not prove demand or represent everyone. [Co-creation][codesign].

## DS-10

**Method:** Concept storyboard. **Prefer when:** An idea's context or sequence is unclear.
**Do:** Sketch trigger, actions, service response and outcome; include a failure branch; annotate what must be true at each transition.
**Output:** An inspectable concept narrative and exposed gaps.
**Needs/limits:** A solution hypothesis; a persuasive story does not establish actual behaviour. [Storyboarding][storyboard].

## DS-11

**Method:** Mechanism prototype. **Prefer when:** One uncertain interaction warrants making tangible.
**Do:** Choose the risky mechanism; build only enough to exercise it: paper flow, letter, support script, physical mock-up, role-play or interactive slice; label simulated parts.
**Output:** Versioned prototype and next evaluation question.
**Needs/limits:** Safe materials/access; making is not validation, and prototype code is not production readiness. [Prototypes][prototypes], [experience prototypes][service-methods].

## DS-12

**Method:** Criteria-based option choice. **Prefer when:** Plausible options require a deliberate trade-off.
**Do:** Agree user value, feasibility, viability and risk criteria; compare options against evidence; expose uncertainties; vary decisive assumptions; record the owner's choice and conditions.
**Output:** Selection rationale and rejected alternatives.
**Needs/limits:** Comparable options; scores support judgement, not objective certainty. [Small-scale selection][framework].

## DS-13

**Method:** Viability stress check. **Prefer when:** An attractive option may be unsustainable.
**Do:** Trace demand to staffing, partners, funding and running/support costs; compare plausible volume/cost ranges; identify capacity limits and ongoing ownership.
**Output:** Viable conditions, resource gaps and no-go triggers.
**Needs/limits:** Grounded estimates; a filled canvas is not a funded business case. [Business model questions][viability].

## DS-14

**Method:** Feasibility spike. **Prefer when:** One technical or operational unknown blocks selection.
**Do:** Define a narrow question and pass/fail criterion; timebox an integration, calculation or process trial; record conditions, result and remaining production work.
**Output:** Feasibility decision with reproducible evidence.
**Needs/limits:** Authorized environment and specialist input; a successful spike proves only its tested conditions. [Alpha feasibility][alpha].

## DS-15

**Method:** Accessibility review. **Prefer when:** Barriers can be found before broader evaluation.
**Do:** Inspect critical tasks for keyboard/focus, structure, labels, contrast, zoom and alternatives; record reproduction, impact, applicable requirement and fix owner.
**Output:** Prioritised barriers and verification scope.
**Needs/limits:** Suitable implementation/tools and competence; quick checks cannot establish full conformance or replace disabled-user research. [W3C checks][accessibility].

## DS-16

**Method:** Expert task walkthrough. **Prefer when:** Known usability principles can expose likely problems.
**Do:** Define role, task and version; inspect steps independently against relevant heuristics; consolidate problems, consequences, disagreements and proposed fixes.
**Output:** Actionable review findings and research questions.
**Needs/limits:** Domain context; predicted difficulty is not observed user failure. [Heuristic evaluation][heuristics].

## DS-17

**Method:** Operational rehearsal. **Prefer when:** A whole service must work across people and systems.
**Do:** Run representative normal, exception and failure scenarios with realistic artifacts; exercise handoffs, support and recovery; compare actual results with acceptance criteria.
**Output:** Verified paths, defects and readiness decision.
**Needs/limits:** Safe environment and relevant roles; rehearsal is not live adoption or production-scale reliability. [Quality assurance][quality].

## DS-18

**Method:** Staged rollout and rollback plan. **Prefer when:** Exposure can grow reversibly.
**Do:** Define initial cohort/channel, version, checks, observation window and expansion thresholds; assign pause authority; rehearse restoring the prior service, including data and communications.
**Output:** Executable release, monitoring and recovery plan.
**Needs/limits:** Actual release authority; planning is not deployment, and some consequences cannot be rolled back. [Deployment][deployment].

## DS-19

**Method:** Live evidence triage. **Prefer when:** A launched change generates mixed operational signals.
**Do:** Verify exposure/version and data quality; compare existing usage, support and incident evidence by context; separate urgent harm from improvement questions; assign action and recheck trigger.
**Output:** Prioritised issues and bounded investigations.
**Needs/limits:** Verified live evidence; missing signals are not success. [Performance context][metrics].

## DS-20

**Method:** Outcome review and loop decision. **Prefer when:** Enough observation exists to judge the original goal.
**Do:** Compare outcomes, baseline and guardrails; examine alternative explanations, segments and costs; choose keep, improve, rollback, research or stop; name the earliest reopened mode.
**Output:** Evidence-linked decision, owner and next action.
**Needs/limits:** Adequate window and trustworthy data; association is not causation. Otherwise record insufficient evidence. [Evaluation][success].
