# Develop ↗ · Solution

Explore materially different mechanisms for the agreed need. Ask: what else could work, which assumption matters, and what small probe could distinguish options? A policy, service, content or operational change is as valid as an interface.

| Question or gap | Candidate | Prefer when… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How are alternative mechanisms understood? | [QL-07 Concept comparison](qualitative.md#ql-07) | Comparably detailed options can be examined against actual use situations. |
| How should information be grouped? | [QL-12 Open card sort](qualitative.md#ql-12) | Users' categories and labels should inform candidate structures. |
| What does the proposed message mean? | [QL-15 Comprehension test](qualitative.md#ql-15) | An instruction, explanation or policy could lead to a wrong action. |
| What character does the design convey? | [QL-16 Desirability elicitation](qualitative.md#ql-16) | Tone or perceived attributes affect a choice; liking is not demand. |
| Does this hierarchy support finding? | [QT-11 Tree testing](quantitative.md#qt-11) | Candidate labels and destinations can be tested independently of layout. |
| Where does someone start? | [QT-12 First-click testing](quantitative.md#qt-12) | Initial visual cues can be compared on a fixed stimulus. |
| Could a later comparison be informative? | [QT-15 Power/MDE feasibility](quantitative.md#qt-15) | Traffic, variation or duration may rule out a decisive experiment. |
| Which reported needs are widespread? | [QT-09 Sampled survey estimation](quantitative.md#qt-09) | Population coverage is defensible; hypothetical preference is not actual adoption. |
| Are these options genuinely different? | [DS-08 Mechanism divergence](design-and-delivery.md#ds-08) | Alternatives vary only cosmetically or assume a new interface. |
| Can affected people reshape an option? | [DS-09 Co-design](design-and-delivery.md#ds-09) | Authorized users/frontline staff can change the proposed mechanism. |
| Is the context or sequence unclear? | [DS-10 Concept storyboard](design-and-delivery.md#ds-10) | A trigger-to-outcome story can expose gaps and failure branches. |
| What must become tangible to learn? | [DS-11 Mechanism prototype](design-and-delivery.md#ds-11) | One uncertain interaction warrants a small, clearly labelled simulation. |

Retain distinct solution hypotheses, their mechanisms, trade-offs and next probes. Reuse evidence from the problem diamond. A convincing storyboard, popular idea or finished prototype is not validation. Choose only the probes that could change selection, then record their actual results and limits in the existing checkpoint.

When making or refining the artifact itself, use the [craft quality bar](../craft-quality.md) for applicable states, copy and rationale; inspection is not user validation.
