Choose research from the decision it needs to inform. Scale depth to uncertainty, consequences and reversibility, and reuse credible existing evidence first.

Need a concrete procedure? Open the [current phase's method menu](../../.agents/skills/diamond-captain/references/methods/README.md). It links 20 qualitative, 20 quantitative and 20 design/delivery methods, each with situations, steps, outputs and limits. For example, diaries suit experiences spread over time; a queue analysis separates waiting from handling. Select only what can change the decision.

For a concrete study, use the relevant part of the [UXR guide](../../.agents/skills/diamond-captain/references/uxr.md). If choosing a tool is still necessary, open the [category index](../../.agents/skills/diamond-captain/references/tool-options.md) and the linked category. ## Plan the smallest useful investigation

Record this chain in the existing task or brief:

**Decision → question → existing evidence → method if needed → review point → finding and owner.**

For example, before replacing an approval interface, determine whether delays arise from routing, missing information or decision rules. A support-case review and timestamps may answer more than a new prototype.

| Question | Useful qualitative evidence | Useful quantitative evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is happening and why might it happen? | Observation, interviews about actual work, support-case review | Funnel, timing, error and segment analysis |
| Which approach works better? | Concept evaluation, usability tests, workflow walkthroughs | Task success, errors and controlled experiments where feasible |
| What changed after launch? | Follow-up interviews, feedback and field observations | Operational metrics, cohorts and experiments where justified |

The methods are candidates, not requirements. They can serve any phase. Use the tools actually available; a named provider in the catalog is not an installed integration.

## Make claims match the evidence

- Interviews can reveal needs and possible explanations; they do not establish population prevalence by themselves.
- A metric difference does not explain its cause. Check definitions, missing data, segments and comparability.
- Before/after movement does not automatically prove the design caused the change. Record rollout context, other changes and uncertainty.
- An illustrative example, proposed study or synthetic test is not actual user research.

## Decide when enough is enough

Agree the question, scope and review point before expanding the investigation. Continue when new evidence could change the decision or reveal material risk. Otherwise, narrow the decision, choose a bounded pilot, defer a question with an owner and revisit trigger, or stop.

Make critical unknowns visible as in scope, deferred, rejected with rationale or blocking. Mandatory requirements remain applicable even when a research activity is optional.

## Record a finding so it can be checked

Include its source, relevant date or period, method and coverage, conclusion, confidence with a reason, limitations and the decision it changes. Confidence is not a substitute for a source. Preserve contradictory findings rather than smoothing them into agreement.
